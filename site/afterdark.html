Kink 101: playing safely

Kink should leave everyone feeling good, before, during and after. Here's what I wish everyone knew before they started. Tap a topic to open it.

I'm sharing what I've learned, not professional, medical or legal advice. When in doubt, slow down and ask.

💗 Consent comes first

Consent is the foundation of everything. An easy way to remember what good consent looks like is FIRES:

  • F
    Freely given: no pressure, guilt or "you owe me".
  • I
    Informed: everyone knows what they're agreeing to, including the risks.
  • R
    Reversible: anyone can change their mind at any time, even mid-scene.
  • E
    Enthusiastic: a real yes, not a shrug or silence.
  • S
    Specific: yes to one thing isn't yes to everything.

You can't consent if you're drunk, high, asleep or being pressured. Being in a relationship doesn't mean automatic consent either.

📝 Negotiate before you play

Talk it through before anything starts, while everyone is clear-headed:

  • What does each person want, and what are their limits?
  • Any injuries, health conditions, medications or triggers to know about?
  • Which safeword or signal will you use?
  • What does each person need afterwards?

If someone won't negotiate or brushes off your limits, that's a red flag. Walk away.

🚦 Safewords and check-ins
  • Traffic lights: Green = all good, Yellow = slow down or check in, Red = stop now, no questions.
  • Non-verbal signals for when someone can't speak: dropping a held object, tapping out three times, or a hand squeeze pattern.
  • Check in often: "What colour are you?" is quick and easy.
  • When a safeword is used, stop immediately and check on them. Never punish or tease someone for using it. A safeword means the system is working.
🧠 Know the frameworks: SSC and RACK
  • SSC: Safe, Sane, Consensual. Play that keeps risk low and minds clear.
  • RACK: Risk-Aware Consensual Kink. Accepts that some play carries risk, and asks that everyone understands and agrees to those risks.

Either way, the point is the same: know the risks, talk about them, agree together.

🎀 Rope and restraint safety
  • Keep safety shears within reach, every time. Rope can't always be untied fast.
  • Never put rope around the neck, and never leave a restrained person alone.
  • Watch for numbness, tingling, coldness or colour changes in hands and feet. Those are nerve and circulation warnings: release right away.
  • Avoid pressure on the inner upper arm, wrists and the back of the knees, where nerves sit close to the skin.
  • Start simple, learn from experienced riggers and classes, and no suspension without proper training.
✋ Impact play basics
  • Stick to fleshy areas like the buttocks and upper thighs.
  • Avoid the spine, kidneys (lower back), tailbone, joints, neck and head.
  • Warm up gently and build slowly. Check in as intensity goes up.
  • Learn each tool on yourself first so you know how it feels.
🕯️ Wax, knives and edge play
  • Wax: use low-temperature body-safe candles (soy or paraffin made for play), never beeswax or regular household candles. Test on yourself first, drip from higher up to cool it, and keep it away from the face and genitals.
  • Knife play is mostly about the mental thrill. Use the dull back of the blade for sensation. If you ever go further, you need sterile tools, first aid knowledge and an agreed limit on any marks.
  • Keep a first aid kit and water nearby, and know where the nearest emergency care is.
  • Edge play means higher risk, so it needs more negotiation, more check-ins and more trust, never less.
🎭 CNC and scene roleplay

Consensual non-consent (CNC) and scenes like kidnapping roleplay look like a lack of consent, but they're built on more consent than almost anything else.

  • Only with someone you already know and trust. Never with a new partner.
  • Negotiate every detail in advance: what's in, what's out, where and roughly when.
  • Because "no" and "stop" may be part of the scene, have a safeword and a non-verbal signal that always end it instantly.
  • Plan extra aftercare. These scenes can bring up big feelings for everyone.
  • Never involve unaware people or public spaces in a scene.
🫂 Aftercare and drop

Intense scenes can leave anyone feeling raw, so plan to look after each other:

  • Water, a snack, a blanket, cuddles or quiet time: whatever each person asked for.
  • Drop (sub drop or top drop) is a low mood that can hit hours or days later. It's normal. Check in the next day too.
  • Aftercare is for everyone involved, not just the person who bottomed.
🛡️ Meeting people safely
  • First meetings happen in public, with no play.
  • Tell a trusted friend where you'll be, and set up a safe call to check in at a set time.
  • Ask around: good partners have good references in the community.
  • No alcohol or drugs during play. Clear heads keep everyone safe.
  • Talk about sexual health and protection honestly, before anything happens.
  • Pressure, rushing, secrecy or "real doms don't need safewords" are all red flags.
🧩 Kink, mental health and disability
  • Share anything that affects your body or mind: chronic pain, mobility limits, PTSD, medications. A good partner adapts the play to you.
  • Agree on what happens if a trigger comes up mid-scene. Stopping is always okay.
  • Kink can be healing, but it isn't therapy. It works best alongside real support.

📖 A quick primer

New to all this? Here's the basic language you'll see below and around the kink community.

Top / Dom
The person leading or "doing" in a scene. A Dom leads with control and structure.
Bottom / sub
The person receiving or following. A sub gives up some control, by choice and on their terms.
Switch
Someone who enjoys both sides, depending on the person or the day.
Scene
A planned period of play, with a clear start and end.
Negotiation
Talking through wants, limits, health and safewords before anything starts.
Safeword
A word or signal that changes or stops the scene immediately, no questions asked.
Headspace
The mental zone someone slips into during play: subspace, pup space, little space and so on.
Aftercare
Looking after each other once the scene ends: water, snacks, cuddles, reassurance.
Soft and hard limits
A soft limit is "maybe, carefully, with the right person". A hard limit is never.

How to start, whatever the kink: learn first (classes, munches, trusted guides), start much lighter than you think you need, negotiate every time, and build trust before intensity. Going slow isn't boring, it's how good play is built.

🎀 Rope play

Using rope to restrain or decorate a partner, from simple wrist ties to Japanese-inspired shibari. For me it's as much about the calm, held feeling as the look.

More about Rope play

What it's like: Calm and grounding for many people. The squeeze of the rope and the slow, careful tying can feel like a full-body hug, and some drift into a floaty, meditative headspace.

Getting started: Start with one simple tie, like a single-column wrist cuff, on an arm or leg. Practise on yourself or a pillow first, and take a beginner rope class or follow a trusted tutorial.

Common gear: Soft rope made for bondage (jute, hemp or cotton, about 6 mm), safety shears, and a comfy surface.

Kept safe by: safety shears nearby, nothing around the neck, regular circulation checks, never leaving someone tied alone.

✋ Impact play

Spanking, paddles, floggers and crops. It ranges from playful and light to intense, and it's all about rhythm and sensation.

More about Impact play

What it's like: A build of warmth, sting or deep thud, depending on the tool. The rhythm, anticipation and endorphins are a big part of the appeal.

Getting started: Begin with a hand, which gives you the most control and feedback. Warm up slowly, use a 1 to 10 scale to check intensity, and add tools later.

Common gear: Your hand, then a paddle, a soft flogger or a crop. Thuddy tools feel gentler than stingy ones.

Kept safe by: warming up, sticking to fleshy areas, avoiding the spine, kidneys and joints, and checking in as intensity rises.

🕯️ Wax play

Dripping warm, melted wax onto skin for a mix of heat, surprise and anticipation. It's also really pretty with coloured wax.

More about Wax play

What it's like: A quick flash of warmth that fades fast, plus anticipation of where the next drop lands. Coloured wax on skin can look beautiful.

Getting started: Test a drop on your own inner arm first. Hold the candle higher for cooler wax and lower for warmer. Start on the back or thighs, never the face.

Common gear: Low-temperature body-safe play candles, a towel or sheet underneath, oil on the skin beforehand for easy clean-up, and water nearby.

Kept safe by: low-temperature body-safe candles only, testing on yourself first, avoiding the face, and agreeing on limits beforehand.

🔪 Knife play

Mostly psychological: the cool edge of a blade and the trust it takes to stay still. It's about fear and thrill, not harm.

More about Knife play

What it's like: Almost entirely mental: the cool edge, the trust it takes to stay still, and the thrill of a little fear in a completely safe setting.

Getting started: Start with the dull back of a blade, or a blunt prop, traced lightly over skin. Lots of trust and negotiation come first, and sharp edges are for experienced players only.

Common gear: A clean blade or a blunt practice knife, a first aid kit, and good lighting.

Kept safe by: using the dull back of the blade, clean tools, a first aid kit nearby, and clear limits on marks (a little is okay, extreme is not).

🐾 Pet play

Taking on the role of a pet, for me a pup (or sometimes a fox!). Playful, affectionate, instinct-driven, with lots of headpats and praise.

More about Pet play

What it's like: Playful, simple and freeing. Words can fall away, and it's all instinct, affection, wiggles and praise. For me it's pup space.

Getting started: Pick your pet and its personality, agree on a few rules (can you talk? use your hands?), and start with short, silly play sessions. Pup groups at Pride and munches are very welcoming.

Common gear: Collar, knee pads, a squeaky toy or ball, and optionally a pup hood, mitts or a tail.

Kept safe by: agreed roles and rules, knee pads for crawling, breaks, and an easy way to step out of the headspace.

⛓️ Collars & leashes

A collar can be a symbol of trust and belonging, a fun pup accessory, or both. A leash adds playful guidance.

More about Collars & leashes

What it's like: A collar can feel like belonging, a hug around the neck, or simply a cute pup accessory. A leash adds playful guidance.

Getting started: Wear a comfortable collar at home first and see how it feels. Talk about what it means to each of you, whether that's play only or a symbol of commitment.

Kept safe by: two fingers' room under the collar, never pulling on the neck, and quick-release clasps.

🪶 Sensory play

Playing with touch and temperature: feathers, ice, soft fur, fingertips. Simple, gentle and surprisingly intense.

More about Sensory play

What it's like: Every touch feels bigger when you focus on it: tickly, shivery, warm or cold. It's gentle and great for beginners.

Getting started: Gather a few textures, take turns, and go slowly. It pairs really well with a blindfold.

Common gear: Feathers, fur mitts, ice cubes, a pinwheel, silk scarves and warm hands.

Kept safe by: checking for allergies, avoiding very cold or hot extremes, and talking about where touch is welcome.

🎭 Consensual non-consent (CNC)

A carefully negotiated scene where resistance is part of the roleplay. It looks like non-consent, but it's built on more consent and trust than almost anything else.

More about Consensual non-consent (CNC)

What it's like: Intense and cathartic for people who enjoy it. It plays with the fantasy of losing control while staying entirely safe underneath.

Getting started: Only with a long-term, deeply trusted partner. Write down the scenario, limits and signals together, and start with a very mild version.

Common gear: Mostly trust and communication. Often a written agreement and a non-verbal safe signal.

Kept safe by: only with a trusted partner, every detail agreed in advance, and a safeword plus a non-verbal signal that always ends the scene.

🚐 Kidnapping roleplay

A scripted "abduction" fantasy, planned together ahead of time, often with a window of when it might happen to keep the surprise.

More about Kidnapping roleplay

What it's like: Adrenaline, surprise and the thrill of being "taken", all scripted and agreed in advance.

Getting started: Agree on a window of dates, safe locations and what happens step by step. Keep everything private and away from the public.

Common gear: A pre-agreed safe signal, a check-in plan, and often a blindfold or soft restraints.

Kept safe by: pre-agreed time, place and limits, never in public or around unaware people, and the same always-works safeword.

🔦 Interrogation scenes

Roleplay built around questioning, "resisting" and eventually giving in. More mental than physical.

More about Interrogation scenes

What it's like: A psychological game of resisting and finally giving in. It's often more about tension and teasing than anything physical.

Getting started: Pick a light theme (secret agent, stolen cookie recipe), agree on topics that are off-limits, and keep it playful at first.

Common gear: A chair, a lamp, maybe light restraints, and a lot of imagination.

Kept safe by: agreed topics that are off-limits, check-ins, and aftercare, since it can stir up real emotions.

🐺 Primal play

Letting go of words and rules to play on instinct: chasing, wrestling, growling, pinning. Raw and very physical.

More about Primal play

What it's like: Raw and freeing: no scripts, just instinct, sounds and physicality. Many people find it a huge stress release.

Getting started: Clear a soft space, agree on off-limit moves and body areas, and pick a tap-out signal that works mid-tussle. Start with play-wrestling.

Common gear: Gym mats or a soft floor, comfy clothes, and sometimes ears or tails.

Kept safe by: a clear space, agreed no-go moves, and a safeword or tap-out that works even mid-tussle.

🦊 Predator & prey

A type of primal play built around the chase: one hunts, one runs, and being caught is the point.

More about Predator & prey

What it's like: The heart-pounding fun of a chase, and the rush of being caught.

Getting started: Use a safe, private space without hazards, agree on boundaries for the chase and what happens after, and keep a clear stop signal.

Common gear: Mostly just space and running shoes. Some people add masks or ears.

Kept safe by: a safe, private space, checking for obstacles, and agreeing what "caught" leads to.

🙈 Sensory deprivation

Blindfolds, earplugs or hoods that take away a sense, making everything else feel stronger.

More about Sensory deprivation

What it's like: Taking away one sense sharpens all the others. Every sound and touch becomes a surprise.

Getting started: Start with just a blindfold for a few minutes while your partner talks to you and stays close.

Common gear: Blindfolds or sleep masks, earplugs or headphones, and only breathable hoods.

Kept safe by: breathable materials only, constant presence of a partner, and a non-verbal signal since words may be harder.

🩺 Medical restraints

Padded cuffs, belts, mitts and straps inspired by hospital-style restraint systems, often part of clinical or medical roleplay. Soft and secure, with a feeling of being completely held in place.

More about Medical restraints

What it's like: Snug, secure and safe, like being firmly tucked in. Some love the clinical, structured roleplay around it.

Getting started: Try a single padded wrist cuff first, then add pieces slowly. Stay in the same room, and agree on how quickly you can be released.

Common gear: Padded restraint sets (cuffs, belts, mitts) with magnetic or key locks, and the key always within reach.

Kept safe by: using real padded restraint gear (not improvised straps), keeping the key or magnet within reach at all times, never leaving the restrained person alone, checking circulation and breathing, never restraining someone face-down, and limiting how long one position is held. Talk about medical trauma first, since hospital settings can be triggering.
