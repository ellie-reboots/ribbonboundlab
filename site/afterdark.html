Kink 101: playing safely

Kink should leave everyone feeling good, before, during and after. Here's what I wish everyone knew before they started. Tap a topic to open it.

I'm sharing what I've learned, not professional, medical or legal advice. When in doubt, slow down and ask.

💗 Consent comes first

Consent is the foundation of everything. An easy way to remember what good consent looks like is FIRES:

  • F
    Freely given: no pressure, guilt or "you owe me".
  • I
    Informed: everyone knows what they're agreeing to, including the risks.
  • R
    Reversible: anyone can change their mind at any time, even mid-scene.
  • E
    Enthusiastic: a real yes, not a shrug or silence.
  • S
    Specific: yes to one thing isn't yes to everything.

You can't consent if you're drunk, high, asleep or being pressured. Being in a relationship doesn't mean automatic consent either.

📝 Negotiate before you play

Talk it through before anything starts, while everyone is clear-headed:

  • What does each person want, and what are their limits?
  • Any injuries, health conditions, medications or triggers to know about?
  • Which safeword or signal will you use?
  • What does each person need afterwards?

If someone won't negotiate or brushes off your limits, that's a red flag. Walk away.

🚦 Safewords and check-ins
  • Traffic lights: Green = all good, Yellow = slow down or check in, Red = stop now, no questions.
  • Non-verbal signals for when someone can't speak: dropping a held object, tapping out three times, or a hand squeeze pattern.
  • Check in often: "What colour are you?" is quick and easy.
  • When a safeword is used, stop immediately and check on them. Never punish or tease someone for using it. A safeword means the system is working.
🧠 Know the frameworks: SSC and RACK
  • SSC: Safe, Sane, Consensual. Play that keeps risk low and minds clear.
  • RACK: Risk-Aware Consensual Kink. Accepts that some play carries risk, and asks that everyone understands and agrees to those risks.

Either way, the point is the same: know the risks, talk about them, agree together.

🎀 Rope and restraint safety
  • Keep safety shears within reach, every time. Rope can't always be untied fast.
  • Never put rope around the neck, and never leave a restrained person alone.
  • Watch for numbness, tingling, coldness or colour changes in hands and feet. Those are nerve and circulation warnings: release right away.
  • Avoid pressure on the inner upper arm, wrists and the back of the knees, where nerves sit close to the skin.
  • Start simple, learn from experienced riggers and classes, and no suspension without proper training.
✋ Impact play basics
  • Stick to fleshy areas like the buttocks and upper thighs.
  • Avoid the spine, kidneys (lower back), tailbone, joints, neck and head.
  • Warm up gently and build slowly. Check in as intensity goes up.
  • Learn each tool on yourself first so you know how it feels.
🕯️ Wax, knives and edge play
  • Wax: use low-temperature body-safe candles (soy or paraffin made for play), never beeswax or regular household candles. Test on yourself first, drip from higher up to cool it, and keep it away from the face and genitals.
  • Knife play is mostly about the mental thrill. Use the dull back of the blade for sensation. If you ever go further, you need sterile tools, first aid knowledge and an agreed limit on any marks.
  • Keep a first aid kit and water nearby, and know where the nearest emergency care is.
  • Edge play means higher risk, so it needs more negotiation, more check-ins and more trust, never less.
🎭 CNC and scene roleplay

Consensual non-consent (CNC) and scenes like kidnapping roleplay look like a lack of consent, but they're built on more consent than almost anything else.

  • Only with someone you already know and trust. Never with a new partner.
  • Negotiate every detail in advance: what's in, what's out, where and roughly when.
  • Because "no" and "stop" may be part of the scene, have a safeword and a non-verbal signal that always end it instantly.
  • Plan extra aftercare. These scenes can bring up big feelings for everyone.
  • Never involve unaware people or public spaces in a scene.
🫂 Aftercare and drop

Intense scenes can leave anyone feeling raw, so plan to look after each other:

  • Water, a snack, a blanket, cuddles or quiet time: whatever each person asked for.
  • Drop (sub drop or top drop) is a low mood that can hit hours or days later. It's normal. Check in the next day too.
  • Aftercare is for everyone involved, not just the person who bottomed.
🛡️ Meeting people safely
  • First meetings happen in public, with no play.
  • Tell a trusted friend where you'll be, and set up a safe call to check in at a set time.
  • Ask around: good partners have good references in the community.
  • No alcohol or drugs during play. Clear heads keep everyone safe.
  • Talk about sexual health and protection honestly, before anything happens.
  • Pressure, rushing, secrecy or "real doms don't need safewords" are all red flags.
🧩 Kink, mental health and disability
  • Share anything that affects your body or mind: chronic pain, mobility limits, PTSD, medications. A good partner adapts the play to you.
  • Agree on what happens if a trigger comes up mid-scene. Stopping is always okay.
  • Kink can be healing, but it isn't therapy. It works best alongside real support.
